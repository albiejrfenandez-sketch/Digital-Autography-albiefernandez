My LOGB Personality

My LOGB Result

My highest score is Golden Retriever (36), making it my dominant personality type. My second-highest score is Beaver (23), making it my sub-dominant type. This suggests that I lead with loyalty, patience, and empathy in how I relate to people, while also carrying a steady, detail-oriented side that keeps me organized and thorough in what I do.

Looking at this result, I think it fits me well. I naturally put a lot of value on being dependable and showing up for the people around me — friends, family, and classmates — even when it's not asked of me. At the same time, my Beaver side shows up when I'm working on something that matters, since I tend to slow down, double-check details, and want things done properly rather than rushed. Between the two, I try to be someone people can rely on, both for support and for getting things right.

04 · Family & Genealogy

An Item From My Grandfather

Inherited · Paternal side

My Grandfather's Boots

These boots were inherited from my grandfather on my father's side of the family. They are important to me because they remind me of the hard work and long days he put in while wearing them.

What makes them more special is that they still carry the marks of real use — scuffs, dirt, and the softened leather that only come from years of walking, working, and standing on his own two feet. He didn't have fancy gear or shortcuts; he just wore what he had and got the job done.

Because of this, the boots are more than just footwear to me. They remind me of my grandfather's strength, resilience, and the quiet dignity of everyday labor. Having these boots makes me feel connected to him and lets me value the grit and memories he left behind.

My OCEAN Test

The OCEAN activity gathered perspectives from a family member (Albie F. Fernandez), my close friends (Jonard Fernandez, Anthony Fernandez, Vince L. Robles), and my new friends (Ezer Guillen, Cyrus Francisco, Royniel Bacalla). Their answers show that people can see different sides of my personality depending on how well they know me and how comfortable I am around them.

O

Openness

I consistently enjoy exploring new or different ideas and respecting other people's values, though my appreciation for art is noticed more often by my close friends than by others.

C

Conscientiousness

I am recognized by everyone for being hardworking and following instructions. While my family member sees me as highly careful and punctual, my friends note that I am occasionally more casual with schedules.

E

Extraversion

Across all groups, I am viewed as consistently talkative and eager for company. I show higher sociability and a more dominant presence around close friends, while appearing a bit more reserved to new acquaintances.

A

Agreeableness

I show a healthy level of independence across all social circles. Rather than simply going along with what others choose, I feel comfortable holding my ground and sharing my own perspective.

N

Neuroticism

Across family, close friends, and new acquaintances alike, I am perceived as balanced and steady, only occasionally experiencing moments of worry or emotional stress.

"People can see me differently depending on the relationship they develop with me and the level of my self-confidence."

What I Learned

One thing I learned from the OCEAN activity is that while core traits like hard work, openness to new ideas, and talkativeness remain steady across my relationships, specific behaviors shift based on familiarity. My family member sees me as extremely punctual and structured, whereas my close friends experience my most social, energetic, and assertive self. New friends observe a slightly quieter dynamic as we build mutual comfort. Overall, this assessment helped me realize how personal trust and relationship depth shape how others perceive and experience my personality.

06 · Final Reflection

Looking Back at Myself

Putting this digital autobiography together gave me a chance to look at my life as a whole story instead of just the version of myself I show day to day. My childhood photos remind me how much I've grown — from being cared for, to playing and exploring, to first learning how to fit into a bigger world at school. My grandfather's boots ground that story in something bigger than me, connecting my own effort and persistence to the hard work he put in long before I was born. The LOGB result reminded me that being loyal and dependable isn't just something I do for others — it's genuinely core to who I am, balanced by a quieter drive to get things right. And the OCEAN activity showed me that the "real me" isn't one fixed version, but something that opens up more the more someone gets to know me. Taken together, these pieces don't just describe who I am — they show me why, and that's something I'll carry forward as I keep growing.
